So one thing that I want to add is that some of the points are that we have been designing apps for users for 10-15 years. The key point here is that the apps were designed for for users like human beings to come to a platform on your app and do the task

But now it's much more crazy. Things have changed. We are one step ahead right now, one step ahead in the sense of humans trying to do the work. Even though the technology and digital apps have made things much smoother by taking something that is physical and creating it digitally

So the physical to digital world meant bringing processes that were done in the physical world to digital so we can freely manipulate it and remove the constraints we had. Things could technically run from wherever you are as a user. You login and do the task.

So naturally the interfaces we built were for humans to talk to computers and do the task.

We could automate a significant chunk here as well.

Now that the agent era is here, it's a layer of abstraction over the digital world.

Meaning we still have the processes mapped out but the important key thing here is that:

We have heard "build the UI for agents" - no, that's incorrect. UI is always built for humans.

Only underlying digital platforms like neo banks and other platforms like ecommerce or banks etc need to build an interface like MCP for agents.

For AI native product companies your value is on the abstraction above.

Your job is to build agents that work one layer up and take on the job like another employee.

So the UI (for humans) using your product basically should change accordingly.

So adding a chatbot won't get you there, since it lacks a few key things:

- It doesn't convey what all the possibilities are well, and it's a stream of text that takes time to read.
